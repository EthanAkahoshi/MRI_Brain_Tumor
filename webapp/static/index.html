    What the model sees

    Every image is cropped to the head and shrunk to 128×128 pixels before the model looks at it. Small details may be lost at this size.

    Reading the heatmap

    Grad-CAM highlights the regions that pushed the model toward its prediction. Red areas mattered most and blue areas least.

    A heatmap shows where the model looked, not whether it was right. If it lights up the skull, background or image edges instead of brain tissue, the model may be relying on the wrong cues.

    The heatmap is coarse (28×28 cells stretched over the image), so treat it as a rough guide, not an outline of a tumour.

    Limitations

    • Trained on about 3,000 public MRI slices, with no patient information or clinical validation.
    • It misses more tumours than it falsely flags. A "No tumor" result is the less reliable of the two.
    • Reported accuracy (about 93%) is likely optimistic: the dataset contains near-duplicate scans.
    • It only says tumour or no tumour. It cannot locate, measure or name a tumour type.
    • It will give an answer for any image, including ones that are not brain MRIs.
