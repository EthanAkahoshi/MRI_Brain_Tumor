        Reading the heatmap

        Grad-CAM highlights the regions that pushed the model toward its answer. Red mattered most, blue least.

        It shows where the model looked, not whether it was right. Heat on the skull, background or image edges means it may be using the wrong cues.

        The heatmap is coarse. It is not a tumour segmentation or an outline of a lesion.

        Limitations

        • Trained on a few thousand public MRI slices, without patient information or clinical validation.
        • It does worse on scans from sources it wasn't trained on, and it can miss tumours. A "No tumour" result never rules one out.
        • Scores are not calibrated probabilities. A 99% score does not mean a 99% chance.
        • It gives an answer for any image, even ones that aren't brain MRIs.
        • Each scan is shrunk to the model's input size, so small details can be lost.
